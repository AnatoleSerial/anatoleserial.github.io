What's up with Roles, anyways?

The Corebook defines the following roles for NPC Mechs:

  • Artillery: Long range damage dealers.
  • Controller: Inflict status effects, conditions or just heat.
  • Defender: Protect areas of the battlefield, or allies.
  • Striker: Non-long range damage dealers.
  • Support: Increase combat capabilities of surrounding allies.

This is a good classification that describes what the NPCs do.

EXCEPT, DO THEY?

Take, for example, the humble Archer, nominally a Controller. Its method of control is choice: most of its Traits involve reactions that force the players to not attack/move/etc. or else they take damage. It is a fun little guy, whose main weapon does Reliable 3 on Tier 1. When played to its strengths, it can either neutralize PCs hard, or deal damage on par or even surpassing an Assault's. Describing it as only a Controller oversimplifies what it can do.

Objective-based Gameplay

Lancer plays a strong emphasis on objective-based combat over the traditional "kill everything on sight" mindset. The problem is that the COrebook's roles don't help in choosing NPCs that fit the objectives.

So, in this humble document, we will create new NPC Role Categories based on their contributions to objective-based combat. Each category comprises two Approaches that help better define what the NPC class Does and How It Does that.

Then we'll take the corebook NPCs and put them in this framework.

The Roles

  • Controller: Mechs that affect the enemies' plans either overtly or indirectly.
    • Infliction Controllers specialize in status effects and heat.
    • Field Controllers adjust the battlefield conditions to their allies' advantage.
  • Striker: Straight up, sometimes gimmicky, damage dealers.
    • Single-Target Strikers' kit focus on damaging individual targets.
    • Multi-Target Strikers are either able to attack multiple targets at once, either separately or as an area of affect.
  • Support: Mechs with heavy limitations, coupled with tricks that aid their allies.
    • Enhancer Supports grant positive buffs to or remove negative effects.
    • Defensive Supports that prevent their allies from suffering damage or adverse effects.
  • Vanguard: Get up close and personal to acquire and contest objectives.
    • Point Vanguards seek to contest and retrieve objectives quickly and aggressively.
    • Denial Vanguards protect objectives, usually by threatening their enemies.

You will notice there is no "Defender" role. This is intentional: that idea has been subsumed by the other roles.
