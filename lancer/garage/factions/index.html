Why factions?

First of all, the main motivation is to make preparing for a game easier. If you have a roster of NPCs ready, whenever there is a need to have an encounter you do not need to scramble to make NPCs. You have a near-complete resource to draw from, with ties to the current narrative.

That ties to the second reason: factions help maintain a cohesive narrative. It can be pretty fun to make some NPC mechs that the players can meet, as adversaries or allies. Factions can give the NPC mechs a distinct identity.

Third point: it is a tried and true technique! Sly Flourish provides some easy to understand general advice on how to create faction rosters for fantasy games, while The Alexandrian provides a more nuanced look in a dungeon-delving context (Along a full account on how the idea for that specific implementation of Faction Rosters came to be -- it's quite a read!).

Of course, this ain't a new concept in LANCER either. Ben Torell at Gordian Blade provides a very good rundown of his own method to create factions. So similar that, in fact, after I wrote my own method down and checked his, a lot of things matched!

It ain't rocket science. But the difference in nuance can be interesting, so I invite you, dear reader, to read both approaches and then make your own.

Anatole's Approach to NPC Factions

I see Factions as a Narrative-first element of the game. This means my first concern is the narrative, while the second is the actual mechanics. Can this lead to unfair sets of NPCs, either too easy or too hard? Of course! Until you go down to the table, you will never know whether a given OPFOR will actually be too weak or too strong, even if it is from a pre-written module, carefully craftted in advanced, or half-improvised using a method like mine.

In the following sections I will go over my method, with an example. Before we begin, let's do the setting for this example.

EXAMPLE: Planet Wanka has has unusual properties (we'll determine them later, during play), so Union and various corps have setup research stations, often unmanned and led by COMP/CON units due to the planet's unusual atmosphere. The first and largest colony is led by William W. Wanka VI, a magnate who has built a transgalactic confectionery empire. He inherited and maintains FACTORY, a colony of thousands of people with next to no oversight from Union due to some administrative issues that occurred during the SecComm era. The PCs have received a rescue signal: a mysterious group of mechanized frames has attacked one of the inhabited Union research stations, stealing an NHP casket from it before heading towards FACTORY.

Preconditions

I make Factions only before a Story. As part of my prep for that particular story, I look at the current state of the narrative, talk to my players about the direction the game is going (i.e. PC projects, plans, etc.), and write down the three most likely NPC Factions that the players may encounter. Each faction has:

  • A Name
  • One or more Objectives
  • One or more Modes of Operation

This informs the kind of actions and approaches the Faction will take with the narrative, and ultimately the kind of mechs they will field.

EXAMPLE: What kind of adversaries can the PCs encounter in Planet Wanka? Let's see.

  • OOMPA REBELS: A group of FACTORY natives who seek to institute democracy by deposing Mr. Wanka. They are a covert operation to avoid Wanka's extensive surveillance network.
  • WANKA CORPS: William W. Wanka VI's currently overextending his ambitions towards ruling the entire planet. He has no qualms with and even prefers direct combat.
  • VERUCA GLOOP: A HORUS cell investigating a potential Metavault, or a similar paracausal phenomenon. They fund their operation by selling technology (their own, or stolen from other big corpros) to both the rebel Oompas and the Wanka Corps.

Creating a Mech Faction

We now know, in general, what these factions are. Now we'll figure out what kind of Mechs they will have.

  1. Write down three Narrative Keywords that describe this Faction's mechs.
  2. For each ROLE, select a Class that best fits this Faction. Based on the keywords, choose a second class for some or all of the ROLES (With a third one class it feels right, but not more). A good idea is to choose classes that have different speeds and ranges within that ROLE.
  3. Select one or two Classes for Grunts. You will be fielding these when you need mass-produced chaff or unimportant combats.
  4. (OPTIONAL) Select one or two preferred templates, besides Ultra, Elite and Veteran. You won't be deploying all the mechs with these templates, but use these if you want to up the ante on an encounter.
  5. (OPTIONAL) Select some optional features. You can either choose features in general, or for a specific class in the roster.

With this, you have an entire roster of potential mechs to deploy.
